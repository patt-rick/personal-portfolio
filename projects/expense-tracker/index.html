Expense Tracker

January 2026 – now · v1.5

Google Play ↗

Expense Tracker keeps track of where your money goes. In Ghana most everyday spending runs through mobile money, and every MoMo payment already sends you an alert, so the app reads those alerts and logs the transaction for you.

Around that: separate cashbooks for different parts of your life, categories, budgets, debts, reports, CSV export and three home-screen widgets. It works offline with no account and no ads, and your data stays on the phone.

Reading the alerts

Native Kotlin code listens for SMS and notifications and queues them, even when the JavaScript side isn't running. A parser of about 1,450 lines matches each message against provider templates (MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash, AirtelTigo, banks and bills) and falls back to a generic parser. Every result gets a weighted confidence score, and anything below the threshold goes to a review queue for you to confirm instead of being saved blindly.

One payment, two alerts

The same payment often arrives as both an SMS and an app notification. Each one is fingerprinted from the sender, currency, amount and the transaction reference when there is one (otherwise the merchant and a two-minute window), and ingestion is serialised per fingerprint so the two can't race each other into a duplicate.

What the money cost to move

Fees and levies are split out of the amount into their own field, so reports show what you spent and, separately, what moving it cost you.

I also ran an adversarial audit of the parser. It found real misreads, like a cashback line being taken as the payment amount, and each one became a regression test. The project has 55 test files, 23 of them for auto-logging.
